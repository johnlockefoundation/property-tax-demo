NC Property Tax Demo — Methodology Notes

Per-property comparison under HB 1089: data sources, benchmark construction, and calculation contract

Version 2.0 · September 2026 · North Carolina fiscal years 2020–21 through 2025–26

1. Purpose

This document describes how the per-property tax comparison on the site is constructed. For a selected residential parcel, the page compares the county property tax actually paid in a single fiscal year, FY2025–26, with what it would have been had the county’s levy been limited to HB 1089: a bankable levy limit anchored to the actual FY2020–21 levy and compounded thereafter by population growth plus inflation.

The comparison holds the parcel’s assessed value constant and applies only the county’s published savings rate, so the result isolates the effect of county rate growth relative to the population-plus-inflation benchmark. County-wide property tax only is compared; municipal, school, and special district taxes are out of scope.

3. Benchmark construction (research methodology)

The hypothetical levy compounds from the FY2020–21 actual county levy:

hyp_t = hyp_(t−1) × (1 + population_growth_t + inflation_t)    for t = FY2021–22, …, FY2025–26, with hyp_0 = FY2020–21 actual

Each subsequent year’s hypothetical levy uses the previous year’s hypothetical levy rather than the actual levy, so the limit compounds over the five-year period. population_growth_t is the county’s annual certified population change (2019→2020, 2020→2021, …, 2023→2024); inflation_t is the corresponding annual change in the U.S. South urban CPI. Municipal and special-district levies are excluded, so the county levies are comparable to a county-wide limit.

3.1 Five-year savings rate

Actual and hypothetical levies are accumulated over FY2021–22 through FY2025–26:

savings_rate = (Σ actual − Σ hypothetical) / Σ actual

Counties at or below the benchmark have a non-positive rate (a grade of A) and are treated as “no savings” on the site. All 100 counties’ computed columns, including this rate, are recorded in Data(tax).csv; column Q (5-year_savings_rate) is the number the receipt displays as “percent lower”.

3.2 Per-property receipt

paid       = V × act / x
could_have  = paid × (1 − savings_rate)
saved      = paid × savings_rate

act is the county’s FY2025–26 actual county-wide levy and x its FY2025–26 assessed valuation base, so paid is the bill the parcel really faced. Because could_have and saved are scaled from paid by the published rate, the dollar values always agree with the printed percentage.

4. Comparison window

The receipt compares one fiscal year (FY2025–26), not the five-year sum. A parcel’s assessment can shift relative to the county tax base over time, so summing years would require assuming the parcel maintains a fixed share of the county base. Comparing the single current year on the current assessment avoids that assumption. The rate shown is the five-year measure from the methodology; the bill shown is single-year.

5. Below-benchmark counties

A county is flagged below benchmark when its FY2025–26 actual levy does not exceed the hypothetical levy (act26 ≤ hyp26). In that case the site shows a short explanation (“would not have saved anything”) instead of a receipt. As of this version the flagged counties are Alamance and Moore.

6. Reconciliation & data notes

  • Bills scale with assessed value. The receipt is V-proportional; a parcel with twice the assessed value sees twice the paid/could-have/saved dollars from the same rate.
  • Parcel values. NC OneMap parcel data is a live snapshot and may predate the 2025 tax year. The county levy and rate figures are the official FY2025–26 records.
  • Residential only. Relief/exemption cases and non-residential parcels are out of scope.
  • Build inputs. Data(tax).csv, the LG04 valuation, and the FIPS map are vendored under data/source/ in this repository; node tools/build_benchmarks.mjs regenerates data/benchmarks.json.

7. Caveats

This is an estimate based on historical tax and property data plus a hypothetical policy scenario. It is not an official tax assessment. The FY2025–26 figures and the underlying population (2019–2024) and inflation (2019–2024) series are subject to the sources’ revisions.
